\section*{Problem and approach}\label{problem-and-approach}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Problem and approach}

Validating a predictive biomarker requires an interaction test. With
two equal subgroups and an interaction as large as the main effect, it
needs about four times the sample of a main-effect test. Aggregated
N-of-1 designs have been proposed to make it affordable, and their
power must be estimated by simulation. Paper 01 asks what the
data-generating process (DGP) of such a simulation should be. It
starts from the DGP of Hendrickson et al.~(2020), the starting DGP, in
which the predictive biomarker is a correlation with the drug
response. It takes the elements of the DGP one at a time, compares the
candidate choices, and judges them by five criteria: existence,
identifiability under carryover, fidelity to the pharmacology,
analyzability and calibration status. The choices are collected as the
recommended DGP, a step toward an optimal one. The Hybrid design is the
primary case. All simulations use the starting response and baseline
parameters of Hendrickson et al.~This summary reproduces every table
and figure of the paper in the paper's order. Each is generated by the
same code as in the paper.

\section{Conclusions}\label{conclusions}

Three choices matter most. First, the biomarker-response correlation
must decay after discontinuation. If it stays constant after first
exposure, the interaction is absent among the post-baseline visits
under any carryover, and power falls nearly to the test size. With a
decaying correlation, power returns to its no-carryover value at
pharmacological half-lives and declines smoothly at half-lives of 0.5
week and above. Second, the serial correlation structure sets the
largest interaction the DGP can hold. A separable AR(1) structure at a
serial correlation of 0.7 raises the bound from 0.256 under compound
symmetry to 0.474. The value 0.7 is a convention, however. At 0.9 the
bound falls to 0.340, and the Hybrid design needs
154 participants at \(c_{bm} = 0.25\)
instead of 278. Third, a matrix outside
the bound should be refused rather than repaired.

At the closed-form sample size, G10 power was 0.844 to 0.936 in the
uncapped cells. We propose the recommended DGP as a reference for
simulation studies of these designs. Every parameter is labeled as a
starting value, a choice or uncalibrated. Calibrating the serial
correlation, the drug-response SD and the symptom half-life is the
next step toward an optimal DGP.
