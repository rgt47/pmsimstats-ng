\section*{Problem and approach}\label{problem-and-approach}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Problem and approach}

Aggregated N-of-1 trials pool repeated within-patient on/off contrasts
to validate predictive biomarkers. Drug carryover attenuates the
biomarker-by-treatment interaction. The analyst must decide how to
represent residual exposure, how to obtain a correctly sized test, and
whether the half-life must be known. We ran an exploratory simulation
study under the recommended DGP, in which the interaction is a
correlation between the biomarker and the drug response and the
biomarker is predictive only. An analysis ladder changes one thing per
rung: the starting analysis E0 (rung 0), a binary indicator with
residual AR(1) correlation G1 (rung 1), CR2 inference G6 (rung 2), an
exposure-weighted predictor at the true half-life G8 (rung 3, an
oracle benchmark), and the half-life chosen by AIC on the model
without the interaction G10 (rung 4). The side analysis G9 chooses the
half-life with the interaction in the model. A biomarker that is also
prognostic is the subject of a companion paper
(1).

\section*{Recommendation}\label{recommendation}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Recommendation}

For a Hybrid trial with a predictive-only biomarker at \(N \le 70\) we
recommend G10: the exposure-weighted predictor with the half-life
chosen by AIC on the model without the interaction, refitted by REML
with the interaction and tested with CR2. Against the oracle at the
true half-life it changes size by -0.002 to +0.002
and power by -0.013 to +0.003 across the families.
Choosing the half-life with the interaction in the model (G9) adds
+0.001 to +0.007 of size and
+0.003 to +0.024 of power. The recommendation is
conditional on the recommended DGP and the parameters simulated.
