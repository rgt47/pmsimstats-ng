\section*{Problem and approach}\label{problem-and-approach}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Problem and approach}

Validating a predictive biomarker requires an interaction test, which
needs about four times the sample of a main-effect test. Hendrickson et
al.~(2020) proposed a data-generating process (DGP) for simulating
aggregated N-of-1 trials. In that original DGP the predictive biomarker
is a correlation with the drug response that is present on drug and
absent off drug. Paper 01 extends the original DGP for the Hybrid
design with carryover. It judges each extension by five criteria:
existence, identifiability under carryover, fidelity to the
pharmacology, analyzability and calibration status. All simulations use
the original response and baseline parameters of Hendrickson et al.
This summary reproduces every table and figure of the paper in the
paper's order. Each is generated by the same code as in the paper.

\section{Conclusions}\label{conclusions}

The correlation encoding of the interaction is coherent. The original
DGP holds the biomarker-response correlation constant after first
exposure, so with any carryover the interaction is absent among the
post-baseline visits and power falls nearly to the test size. The
extended DGP lets the correlation decay
after discontinuation, and power returns to its no-carryover value at
pharmacological half-lives. The original effect size of 0.6 relied on
repaired matrices, and a separable AR(1) structure at serial
correlation 0.7 raises the feasibility bound to 0.474. At the
closed-form sample size, G10 power was 0.844 to 0.936 in the
uncapped cells. We propose the extended DGP, with a stop on infeasible
matrices, as the reference for simulation studies of these designs.
