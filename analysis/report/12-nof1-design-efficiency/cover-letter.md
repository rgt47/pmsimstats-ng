# Cover letter
*2026-08-20*

To the Editor,
Statistics in Medicine

Dear Editor:

We are pleased to submit our manuscript, 'A Closed-Form
Approximation to the Biomarker-Treatment Interaction Estimator in
Aggregated N-of-1 Trials, and What It Implies for Design Efficiency
Under a Mandatory Open-Label Lead-In,' for consideration as a short
communication in Statistics in Medicine.

Aggregated N-of-1 trials targeting a biomarker-treatment interaction
have so far been evaluated almost exclusively by Monte Carlo
simulation; no closed-form expression exists for the power of the
mixed-model interaction test under carryover, in contrast to the
treatment main effect, for which Senn's and Zucker's classical
variance formulas have long provided design intuition. This absence
is a practical problem for trialists: without a closed form, every
design question (how many randomization paths, what assessment
timing, how much a given carryover half-life costs) requires a fresh
simulation.

This short communication derives a closed-form approximation to the
sampling variance and power of the interaction estimator, obtained by
reducing the fixed analysis model to a within-subject two-group
contrast and applying the exact variance of a mean of AR(1)-correlated,
unequally spaced observations, together with a companion carryover
attenuation factor derived from each design's visit schedule. We
validate both against Monte Carlo output already produced for a
companion manuscript in this series, reporting honestly where the
approximation succeeds (it explains 95% of the variance in the
simulated sampling variance across three designs, three carryover
half-lives, and two sample sizes with a single fitted scale
parameter, and predicts simulated power to within a mean absolute
error of 0.05) and where it falls short (the attenuation factor
correctly ranks designs by carryover vulnerability but overstates
its magnitude relative to the fitted mixed model, which recovers
information the crude contrast discards). We then use the validated
components to give quantitative, closed-form-grounded design guidance
for the common practical scenario in which the trial's first phase
must be open-label for recruitment reasons, a fixed constraint the
compendium of companion manuscripts had previously addressed only
qualitatively.

We believe this contribution is well suited to a short communication:
it is a single, sharply scoped result (a closed form and its honest
validation) rather than a new simulation study, and it directly
serves trialists designing the next aggregated N-of-1 study in this
series' target application (biomarker-stratified N-of-1 trials in
PTSD pharmacotherapy) and in the broader precision-medicine
literature this series engages.

This manuscript has not been published previously and is not under
consideration elsewhere. All authors have approved the submission.

Sincerely,
The pmsimstats team
