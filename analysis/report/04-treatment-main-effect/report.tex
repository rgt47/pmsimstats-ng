\begin{abstract}
\textbf{Background.} Aggregated N-of-1 trials are proposed as an efficient alternative to parallel-group trials. Published simulations compare them at matched participant counts but not at matched observation counts. Carryover handling is rarely compared across rules that a trial can apply.
\textbf{Methods.} We simulated a linear data-generating process for prazosin and PTSD nightmares. The N-of-1 arm was a randomized 4 + 4 multi-crossover of one-week periods in 35 participants. Five analysis rules handled carryover. Three parallel-group comparators used 35 participants. We derived power in closed form and planned 2,000 replicates per cell to confirm it.
\textbf{Results.} The closed form gave power 0.84 for the schedule-exclusion rule and 0.24 for a one-visit trial at an effect of -1 nightmare per week. A trial with eight visits and a noisy baseline reached 0.63. Under a true half-life of one week, schedule exclusion had bias 0.20 and coverage 0.91. The simulation confirmation is pending.
\textbf{Conclusions.} Most of the apparent N-of-1 advantage reflects the number of observations per participant. The remaining advantage is smaller and depends on how carryover is handled. This pilot does not simulate the hybrid design, treatment-effect heterogeneity, or a placebo response.
\textbf{Keywords:} N-of-1 trials; randomized controlled trials; statistical power; carryover; crossover design; Monte Carlo simulation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{introduction}

Aggregated N-of-1 trials combine several crossover series, one per participant, in a single analysis\textsuperscript{\citeproc{ref-zucker1997combining}{1},\citeproc{ref-guyatt1986determining}{2}}. Each participant serves as the control. This removes between-participant variance from the treatment contrast\textsuperscript{\citeproc{ref-duan2013singlepatient}{3},\citeproc{ref-schmid2022bayesian}{4}}. Simulation studies report that aggregated N-of-1 designs reach a given power with fewer participants than parallel-group trials\textsuperscript{\citeproc{ref-blackstonComparisonAggregatedNof12019}{5},\citeproc{ref-carrozzoTwoArm2025}{6}}.

This paper asks how much of that advantage is due to the design and how much to the number of observations per participant. It also asks how the choice of carryover rule changes the answer. The setting is prazosin for trauma-related nightmares in PTSD. The first randomized trials of prazosin were positive\textsuperscript{\citeproc{ref-raskind2003}{7},\citeproc{ref-raskind2013}{8}}. A later larger trial in veterans found no difference from placebo on its primary outcomes\textsuperscript{\citeproc{ref-raskind2018}{9}}. The prior evidence for the effect size is therefore mixed. The effect sizes in this paper are chosen for illustration.

\subsection{Scope of this paper}\label{scope-of-this-paper}

This paper is a pilot. It uses a linear data-generating process (DGP) and a randomized multi-crossover design. It does not simulate the Hybrid design of Hendrickson et al.\textsuperscript{\citeproc{ref-hendricksonOptimizing2020}{10}}, treatment-effect heterogeneity, a placebo response, or a natural-history trend. The working protocol for a fuller study (\texttt{00-ademp-protocol.md}) describes those components. That study has not been run. The protocol that governs the present pilot is \texttt{00-ademp-protocol-v1.md}. The section on deviations lists every difference between that protocol and what was done.

The study is exploratory. No hypothesis in it was specified before the data were generated, and its findings need confirmation in an independent study.

\subsection{Prior simulation comparisons}\label{prior-simulation-comparisons}

Table \ref{tab:prior} summarizes the closest published simulations. Blackston et al.~report 92\% power for aggregated N-of-1 trials, 32\% for a parallel-group trial, and 50\% for a two-period crossover at \(n = 30\) in their first variance scenario\textsuperscript{\citeproc{ref-blackstonComparisonAggregatedNof12019}{5}}. Their parallel-group trial also supplies one observation per participant. The observation-count difference is therefore present in their design as well. Percha et al.~simulate N-of-1 series with carryover, wash-in, and autocorrelation, and give design recommendations on block count, order, period length, and sampling frequency\textsuperscript{\citeproc{ref-percha2019designing}{11}}. Wang and Schork give analytical power under AR(1) errors, heteroscedasticity, washout, and carryover\textsuperscript{\citeproc{ref-wang2019}{12}}.

\subsection{Closed-form power}\label{closed-form-power}

Senn gives the variance of the mean treatment difference for a series of paired cycles as \((\tau^2 + 2\sigma^2/k)/n\) for \(n\) participants and \(k\) cycles\textsuperscript{\citeproc{ref-sennSampleSizeConsiderations2019}{13}}. Here \(\tau^2\) is the between-participant variance of the drug effect and \(\sigma^2\) is the within-period variance. Yang et al.~extend closed-form planning for N-of-1 series\textsuperscript{\citeproc{ref-yang2021sample}{14}}. The DGP in this paper has \(\tau^2 = 0\). The closed form therefore applies directly, and the Methods derive it. The simulation confirms it and checks the finite-sample behavior of the inference.

\subsection{Objectives}\label{objectives}

The objectives were fourfold.

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  To derive the power of each analysis arm in closed form.
\item
  To compare a multi-crossover N-of-1 design with parallel-group comparators of one visit and of eight visits, at 35 participants.
\item
  To compare five carryover rules, including one that uses the truth and is labeled as such.
\item
  To confirm the closed form and the Type I error by Monte Carlo simulation.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Aims}\label{aims}

The aim was to quantify power, bias, coverage, and Type I error for a treatment main effect in a multi-crossover N-of-1 design and in parallel-group comparators, and to show how the carryover rule changes them.

\subsection{Data-generating mechanisms}\label{data-generating-mechanisms}

\textbf{Units.} Time is in weeks. One period is one week. The carryover half-life \(t_{1/2}\) is in weeks. One week is seven days.

Each replicate draws \(N = 35\) participant intercepts \(u_i \sim \mathcal N(0, \sigma_u^2)\) with \(\sigma_u = 1.5\). The same participants enter every arm of that replicate. The baseline mean is \(\mu_0 = 8\) nightmares per week.

In the N-of-1 arm, participant \(i\) completes \(T = 8\) weekly periods, four on drug and four on placebo, in a random order. The outcome of period \(t\) is

\[Y_{it} = \mu_0 + u_i + \beta_D D_{it} + \beta_D\, c_{it} + \varepsilon_{it}.\]

Here \(D_{it}\) is the drug indicator and \(\beta_D\) is the on-drug effect. The residuals \(\varepsilon_{it}\) have standard deviation \(\sigma_\varepsilon = 2.3\) and lag-one correlation \(\rho = 0.3\) per week, so that \(\mathrm{Cor}(\varepsilon_{it}, \varepsilon_{is}) = \rho^{|w_t - w_s|}\). The treatment-effect heterogeneity is \(\tau^2 = 0\). The intraclass correlation of one measurement is \(\sigma_u^2/(\sigma_u^2 + \sigma_\varepsilon^2) = 0.30\).

The carryover term \(c_{it}\) is zero on drug and before first exposure. In an off-drug period after exposure it is the mean of \(2^{-s/t_{1/2}}\) over the period,

\[c_{it} = \frac{t_{1/2}}{\ln 2}\left(2^{-k_{it}/t_{1/2}} - 2^{-(k_{it}+1)/t_{1/2}}\right),\]

where \(k_{it}\) is the number of completed off-drug periods since the last drug period. The carryover decays from the last on-drug mean and continues across consecutive off-drug periods. A half-life of zero means no carryover.

The primary half-life is 0.2 week, inside the pharmacological range for prazosin. Its plasma half-life of 2 to 3 hours\textsuperscript{\citeproc{ref-minipress}{16}} is shorter, so 0.2 week is a conservative allowance for pharmacodynamic persistence. Longer half-lives of 0.5, 1, and 2 weeks are behavioral scenarios. They are not pharmacological estimates.

Table \ref{tab:calib} lists the parameters. The values of \(\mu_0\), \(\sigma_u\), \(\sigma_\varepsilon\), \(\rho\), and the effect grid are assumptions chosen to be of plausible size. They are not estimated from Raskind et al.\textsuperscript{\citeproc{ref-raskind2013}{8}} or Hendrickson et al.\textsuperscript{\citeproc{ref-hendricksonOptimizing2020}{10}}. The first of these measured nightmares with the Clinician-Administered PTSD Scale dream item and not with weekly counts.

\textbf{Parallel-group comparators.} The same 35 participants are allocated 17 to 18 at random. Three comparators differ in visits and in the baseline covariate.

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  RCT1\_exact: one post-baseline visit, ANCOVA on the true intercept \(u_i\). This is the comparator of the earlier version of this paper.
\item
  RCT1\_r08: one visit, ANCOVA on a baseline covariate with reliability \(r_{BL} = 0.8\).
\item
  RCT8\_r08: eight weekly visits, ANCOVA on the participant mean of the eight visits, with the same covariate. For balanced complete data this equals the generalized least squares estimator of the mixed model. It matches the N-of-1 arm in the number of observations per participant.
\end{itemize}

A covariate with reliability \(r_{BL}\) is \(u_i\) plus independent noise of variance \(\sigma_u^2(1 - r_{BL})/r_{BL}\). A single baseline visit from this DGP would have reliability equal to the intraclass correlation above. The value 0.8 is therefore a favorable scenario for the parallel-group trial.

\subsection{Estimand}\label{estimand}

The estimand is the on-drug effect \(\beta_D\) in nightmares per week. In the N-of-1 arm it is the direct effect of drug during an on-drug period. Power is the probability that the two-sided test of \(\beta_D = 0\) rejects at the 0.05 level.

\subsection{Analysis methods}\label{analysis-methods}

Each N-of-1 rule fits

\[Y_{it} = \beta_0 + \beta_D\, x_{it} + \gamma\, L_{it} + \beta_T\, w_t + u_i + \varepsilon_{it}\]

with a random intercept and a continuous-time AR(1) residual correlation (\texttt{nlme::lme}, \texttt{corCAR1}, REML). The term \(\gamma L_{it}\) enters only in the DBCL rule. Table \ref{tab:rules} defines the five rules.

The rule XS uses the schedule only and is applied identically in the null and alternative cells. The rule XO is an oracle. It uses the true half-life, and it is reported only to show what perfect knowledge would give. The rule \(D_{bc}\) is the drug indicator in on-drug periods and the carryover fraction at \(t_{1/2}^a\) in off-drug periods after exposure. It is zero before first exposure.

Inference uses two methods for each rule. The first is the \texttt{nlme} Wald \(t\)-test with containment (between-within) degrees of freedom. The second is CR2 cluster-robust standard errors with Satterthwaite degrees of freedom\textsuperscript{\citeproc{ref-pustejovsky2018small}{17}}. The \texttt{nlme} package has no Satterthwaite option. The earlier version of this paper called its containment-df test Satterthwaite. That description was wrong.

\subsection{Performance measures}\label{performance-measures}

For every arm and cell we report power, bias, empirical standard error, mean model standard error, and 95\% interval coverage, each with its Monte Carlo standard error (MCSE). The MCSE of a proportion \(\hat p\) is \(\sqrt{\hat p(1-\hat p)/n_{\mathrm{sim}}}\). The paired difference in power between two arms uses the replicate-level rejection indicators. Its MCSE is the standard deviation of the indicator difference divided by \(\sqrt{n_{\mathrm{sim}}}\).

\subsection{Closed-form performance}\label{closed-form-performance}

Under the DGP, the covariance of one participant's outcomes is \(V = \sigma_u^2 \mathbf 1\mathbf 1^\top + \sigma_\varepsilon^2 R(\rho)\), with \(R_{ts} = \rho^{|t-s|}\). For a rule with retained rows and regressor matrix \(X_i\), the generalized least squares variance of \(\hat\beta_D\) is the \(\beta_D\) element of \((\sum_i X_i^\top V_i^{-1} X_i)^{-1}\). We average the information over the 70 equally likely balanced sequences. The expected estimate is the same inverse times \(\sum_i X_i^\top V_i^{-1}\mu_i\), where \(\mu_i\) is the true mean including carryover. The difference from \(\beta_D\) is the bias. Power and coverage follow from the noncentral \(t\) distribution with the containment degrees of freedom and noncentrality \((\beta_D + \mathrm{bias})/\mathrm{SE}\).

For the parallel-group comparators, the residual variance after covariate adjustment is \(\sigma_\varepsilon^2\, s + \sigma_u^2(1 - r_{BL})\), with \(s = \mathbf 1^\top R \mathbf 1/m^2\) for \(m\) visits. The variance of the effect is that residual variance times \(1/17 + 1/18\).

The closed form ignores the sampling error of the estimated variance components. The simulation measures that error.

\subsection{Simulation size and random numbers}\label{simulation-size-and-random-numbers}

Each cell has \(n_{\mathrm{sim}} = 2{,}000\) replicates. At this size the MCSE of a power near 0.75 is 0.010, and of a rejection rate near 0.05 it is 0.005. There are two sweeps. The M1 effect sweep has \(\beta_D \in \{0, -0.5, -1, -2\}\) at \(t_{1/2} = 0.2\). The S1 half-life sweep has \(t_{1/2} \in \{0, 0.5, 1, 2\}\) at \(\beta_D = -1\). The carryover term is proportional to \(\beta_D\), so a null cell does not depend on \(t_{1/2}\).

One L'Ecuyer-CMRG chain, advanced with \texttt{parallel::nextRNGStream()}, gives every replicate in every cell its own stream. The streams are stored, and results do not depend on the number of cores. This replaces the earlier driver, in which \texttt{mclapply} reused one set of streams in all nine cells.

\subsection{Software}\label{software}

The analyses use R 4.4 or later with \texttt{nlme}, \texttt{clubSandwich}, and \texttt{parallel}. The code is in \texttt{analysis/scripts/treatment-main-effect/}. The file \texttt{pilot-core.R} holds the DGP, the analysis rules, and the closed form. The file \texttt{production-run.R} is the driver, and \texttt{check-pilot.R} runs the fast checks. Package versions are in \texttt{renv.lock}.

\section{Discussion}\label{discussion}

\subsection{Principal findings}\label{principal-findings}

Under this linear DGP, power is a deterministic function of the two variance components, the lag-one correlation, the participant count, the design, and the carryover rule. The closed form reproduces the power of the stored v1 run to within 0.03. The standard error of the one-visit comparator is 2.31 times that of XS. Most of that ratio reflects the number of observations per participant. Giving the parallel-group trial eight visits and a covariate with reliability 0.8 reduces the ratio to 1.26.

The multi-crossover still has higher power than the matched-burden comparator at \(\beta_D = -1\). The power is 0.84 for XS and 0.93 for DBC, against 0.63. Two features of the DGP limit this comparison. It has no placebo response, so the blinded structure of the parallel-group trial earns no credit. It also has no period or order effect, so the randomized order of the N-of-1 arm is not tested.

\subsection{Carryover}\label{carryover}

The schedule rule XS is nearly unbiased at the pharmacological half-life and biased at behavioral half-lives of 0.5 week or longer. The rule DBC has the best power at 0.2 week, which is the half-life it assumes. Among the rules that handle carryover, it has the largest bias at one week and longer. The lag regressor in DBCL reduces that bias to the level of XS at a cost in standard error. The oracle shows that unbiasedness at long half-lives requires dropping most periods. Our results on carryover depend on the DGP's assumption that carryover is an exponential decay of the on-drug effect. Other shapes were not simulated.

An earlier version of this paper reported that period exclusion was robust across half-lives. That result was an identity of the oracle rule, which dropped contaminated periods whatever the half-life. It is not a property of any rule that a trial can apply.

\subsection{Comparison with prior studies}\label{comparison-with-prior-studies}

Blackston et al.~found higher power for aggregated N-of-1 trials than for a parallel-group trial, and noted inflated Type I error under unmodeled carryover\textsuperscript{\citeproc{ref-blackstonComparisonAggregatedNof12019}{5}}. The present closed form agrees on direction. Our DGP does not reproduce their Type I inflation because carryover is proportional to \(\beta_D\), so it vanishes under the null. A design with a carryover that is independent of the treatment effect would need a different DGP. Chen and Chen compared four analyses, including a mixed model with a carryover regressor\textsuperscript{\citeproc{ref-chen2014four}{18}}. The DBCL rule is of that kind. Tang and Landes show that single-participant tests need corrections for serial correlation\textsuperscript{\citeproc{ref-tangTtestsNof1Trials2020}{19}}. In their Table 2, with four observations, the effect detectable with 80\% power rises from \(1.65\sigma\) at \(\rho = 0\) to \(4.08\sigma\) at \(\rho = 0.4\) and \(13.73\sigma\) at \(\rho = 0.6\). Those values concern one participant and a one-sided 0.05 level. They do not apply to the aggregated analysis here, where 35 participants share the estimation of \(\rho\).

\subsection{Limitations}\label{limitations}

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  The DGP is linear with Gaussian errors. Nightmare counts are nonnegative integers.
\item
  There is no treatment-effect heterogeneity (\(\tau^2 = 0\)), so individual effects are undefined and the Senn trade-off between participants and cycles\textsuperscript{\citeproc{ref-sennSampleSizeConsiderations2019}{13}} is not examined.
\item
  There is no placebo response, natural-history trend, period effect, dropout, or missing data.
\item
  The design is a multi-crossover. It is not the Hybrid design.
\item
  The parameters are assumed. They are not estimated from prazosin trials.
\item
  Confirmatory simulation results are pending. The closed form ignores the sampling error of the estimated variance components.
\item
  Both arms share one participant pool within a replicate, so arm comparisons within a cell are paired. Different cells use independent streams.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusions}\label{conclusions}

In a linear DGP at 35 participants, a randomized multi-crossover has higher power than a one-visit parallel-group trial mainly because it observes each participant several times. Matching the number of visits removes most of the standard-error gap. The remaining advantage depends on the carryover rule. At a pharmacological half-life the schedule rule is nearly unbiased, and at behavioral half-lives of 0.5 week or longer every applicable rule is biased or loses power. The Monte Carlo confirmation of these closed-form results is pending.

\section{Glossary}\label{glossary}

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{ALL, XS, DBC, DBCL, XO.} The five N-of-1 analysis rules in Table \ref{tab:rules}.
\item
  \textbf{Analysis half-life \(t_{1/2}^a\).} The half-life the analysis assumes when it builds \(D_{bc}\). The policy value is 0.2 week.
\item
  \textbf{Carryover fraction.} The mean over a period of \(2^{-s/t_{1/2}}\), the share of the on-drug effect that remains.
\item
  \textbf{CR2.} The bias-reduced cluster-robust variance estimator with Satterthwaite degrees of freedom.
\item
  \textbf{Containment df.} The between-within degrees of freedom that \texttt{nlme} reports for a within-participant term.
\item
  \textbf{\(D_{bc}\).} The drug indicator extended by the carryover fraction in off-drug periods after exposure.
\item
  \textbf{Hybrid design.} Open-label titration, then blinded discontinuation, then a brief crossover. It is not simulated here.
\item
  \textbf{Multi-crossover.} A randomized order of equal numbers of drug and placebo periods within each participant.
\item
  \textbf{Oracle.} A rule that uses the true half-life. XO is the only oracle in this paper.
\item
  \textbf{RCT1\_exact, RCT1\_r08, RCT8\_r08.} The three parallel-group comparators defined in the Methods.
\item
  \textbf{Reliability \(r_{BL}\).} The share of the variance of the baseline covariate that is due to the true participant intercept.
\item
  \textbf{\(t_{1/2}\).} The carryover half-life of the DGP, in weeks.
\end{itemize}

\section{Reproducibility}\label{reproducibility}

The random-number scheme is one L'Ecuyer-CMRG chain seeded with \texttt{20261028}, advanced with \texttt{parallel::nextRNGStream()}. Replicate \(i\) of cell \(c\) uses stream number \((c-1) \times 2000 + i\) of that chain, and each replicate sets its stream explicitly. The production run stores one row per replicate and rule. The per-replicate streams can be regenerated from the seed with \texttt{make\_streams()} in \texttt{pilot-core.R}. Simulation results depend on the platform's BLAS and LAPACK in the last digits.

The fast checks (\texttt{check-pilot.R}) cover the structure of the rules, the agreement of the closed form with the stored 2026-05-19 run, the agreement of the closed form with a 60-replicate simulation, and the reproducibility of the streams. They run in about 15 seconds. They do not replace the production run.
