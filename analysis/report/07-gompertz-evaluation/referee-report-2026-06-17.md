# Referee report: Critical evaluation of the modified Gompertz response function as a parametric template for treatment-response trajectories in N-of-1 trial simulation
*2026-06-17 08:02 PDT*

Target venue (per cover letter): *Statistics in Medicine*, Methods Note / Tutorial track.
Manuscript source: `analysis/report/07-gompertz-evaluation/report.Rmd`.

---

## 1. Summary of the manuscript

The manuscript asks whether the choice of parametric family for the
latent treatment-response trajectory, embedded in the pmsimstats
Monte Carlo engine for aggregated N-of-1 power analysis, materially
affects inference about a biomarker-by-treatment interaction. The
framework's default is a modified, monotone-saturating Gompertz
function applied to each of three latent components (biological
response BR, placebo-belief PB, natural-history TV). The authors
position the work as importing family-comparison sensitivity
apparatus from oncology and pharmacometrics into an N-of-1
simulation literature they argue is silent on the question. They
report a 16-cell focused factorial (four trajectory families x two
DGP architectures x two biomarker effect-size levels) at N = 35,
OL+BDC design, zero carryover, 1,000 replicates per cell, analysed
throughout with the standard pmsimstats linear mixed model
(`Sx ~ bm + t + Dbc + bm:Dbc + (1|ptID)`, `corCAR1`). The headline
result is a clean null: power (~0.76 to 0.81 at c.bm = 0.45), type I
error (0.025 to 0.036), and interaction-estimate bias are
statistically indistinguishable across the four families under both
architectures. The authors conclude that the Gompertz default is
defensible at the reference cell and that sensitivity-analysis effort
is better spent on carryover and DGP-architecture axes.

## 2. Overall assessment and recommendation

**Recommendation: Major revision (bordering on reject-and-resubmit).**

The manuscript is honest, clearly motivated, and addresses a real gap.
The numerical results reported in the tables faithfully reproduce the
committed simulation output (I verified every cell against
`07-gompertz-summary.txt`). However, the central empirical claim does
not follow from the experiment as actually implemented. The "four
trajectory families" are not four data-generating processes. Inspection
of the driver (`01-architecture-a-trajectory-sweep.R`, lines 162-248)
shows that all data, under every cell, are generated by the Gompertz
MVN engine, and the alternative "family" is then imposed as a
deterministic, subject-invariant additive shift to the BR mean at each
timepoint (`shift = family(tod) - gompertz(tod)`). That shift does not
depend on the subject, does not depend on the biomarker, and never
enters the covariance structure or the interaction-generating
machinery. Because the estimand is the biomarker-by-treatment
interaction, a manipulation that is constant across subjects and
orthogonal to the biomarker cannot, by construction, move the
`bm:Dbc` sampling distribution by more than mean-structure leverage
that is largely absorbed by the `t` and `Dbc` fixed effects. The null
result is therefore close to a mathematical consequence of the
implementation rather than an empirical finding about trajectory
families. This must be fixed before the paper's conclusion can be
believed, and the fix requires re-running the study with genuinely
family-specific data generation. Two further problems compound this:
the identifiability/"sloppiness" analysis that the introduction and
the pre-registration advertise as a pillar of the contribution is
absent from the Results entirely; and two of the four "families"
(logistic and hyperbolic-tangent) are the same two-parameter sigmoid
family up to a rate reparameterisation, which the manuscript itself
half-acknowledges yet treats as distinct. For a Methods Note whose
sole deliverable is a sensitivity comparison, these are not peripheral.

A negative result is publishable and valuable. But it must be a
negative result about the question posed, produced by an experiment
capable of returning a positive one. The current experiment is not.

## 3. Significance and novelty

The novelty claim, that the published N-of-1 methodology corpus does
not report trajectory-family-misspecification sensitivity, is
plausible and survived my literature checks. Searches for N-of-1
simulation with response-curve-family misspecification returned
nothing on point; the adjacent comparative apparatus the authors cite
(Benzekry 2014 eight-model tumour-growth comparison; Tjorve unified-
Richards; Jagadeesan sloppiness) is real and correctly characterised.
The closest threats to novelty are general growth-curve selection and
misspecification studies (e.g. tumour-growth model-selection work, and
Gompertz-vs-logistic selection tests), but none is situated in the
N-of-1 power-analysis setting, so the framing gap is genuine.

The significance, as delivered, is modest and is undercut by the
implementation problem in Section 2. If the family manipulation were
genuine and the null still held, the contribution would be a useful,
if narrow, reassurance for one application (prazosin-PTSD) at one cell.
That is appropriate for a Methods Note but not more. The manuscript's
own framing promises more than it delivers: the introduction builds an
extended case around Gompertz identifiability fragility in short noisy
series (Section "Why the question matters", second sub-argument;
Jagadeesan sloppiness; Tjorve reparameterisation), and the design
section states that "Study 2 is the sloppiness eigenvalue diagnostic",
yet no identifiability or sloppiness result appears anywhere in the
Results, Discussion, or Conclusions. The paper currently advertises a
two-pronged contribution (inferential robustness + identifiability
diagnosis) and delivers only the first prong, and that one in
compromised form.

## 4. Major comments

**M1. The trajectory "family" is implemented as a subject-invariant
additive mean shift on Gompertz-generated data, so the null result is
largely built in.** *(Inspected: driver lines 162-248; verified
against the data-generating logic.)*
This is the decisive issue. In `run_one`, every replicate of every
cell is generated by `generateData(..., dgp_architecture = arch)`,
which is the Gompertz-based engine, including the biomarker coupling
(Architecture B covariance block, or Architecture A mean moderation
`c.bm * bm_z * br_sd`). Only afterwards, for non-Gompertz families, is
a fixed per-timepoint offset `shift_g[tp] = family(tod) - gompertz(tod)`
added to the BR column and propagated to the outcome. This offset is
identical across all subjects and all replicates within a path and is
not a function of the biomarker. Consequently the alternative families
never drive the subject-level response, the residual covariance, or
the interaction signal; they perturb only the marginal BR mean
trajectory by a deterministic constant at each timepoint, most of
which is collinear with the `t` and `Dbc` fixed effects (8 timepoints,
limited fixed-effect degrees of freedom). It is therefore expected
*a priori*, not discovered, that `bm:Dbc` power and bias are invariant.
The paper's prose ("we generate trajectories under each of the four
families and analyse them under the standard model", Section 1 and
Section 4) describes a genuine four-DGP design that the code does not
implement. *Why it matters:* the headline conclusion ("trajectory
family choice has no detectable effect") is not supported by an
experiment capable of detecting such an effect; it is an artefact of an
inert manipulation. *Remedy:* re-implement each family as a true
data-generating process in which the family governs the subject-level
BR trajectory and, critically, the biomarker-by-treatment coupling
(for Architecture A, apply the moderation to the family's own mean
function, not to Gompertz; for Architecture B, the family must shape
the BR block that carries the differential correlation). Re-run and
report. If the null survives a genuine manipulation, the paper becomes
sound. Until then, the manuscript should not claim a substantive null.

**M2. Logistic and hyperbolic-tangent are the same two-parameter
family; they cannot constitute independent "families".** *(Verified
algebraically.)* The manuscript states (Section 3, opening of the
hyperbolic-tangent subsection, and again at the contribution statement)
that tanh is "formally a rescaled symmetric logistic", while
simultaneously claiming tanh "spans the fast-saturating smooth regime
that neither the logistic nor the Gompertz family can span". Both
cannot be true. `(m/2)(1 + tanh(r(t - t0))) = m / (1 + exp(-2r(t - t0)))`
is exactly the logistic with rate `2r`. With the asymptote matched and
the rate free, the logistic family contains the tanh curve. The driver
fixes `rate = 0.5` for both and solves the offset (logistic t0 = 4.295,
tanh t0 = 4.829), so the two trace different *curves*, but they remain
one *family* differing only by a rate factor of two. The design thus
has at most three genuinely distinct shape families (asymmetric
Gompertz; symmetric sigmoid; piecewise-linear), not four. *Why it
matters:* the claim that the four families "span the qualitative range
of plausible templates" is overstated, and one of the four columns is
informationally redundant. *Remedy:* either drop tanh and replace it
with a genuinely distinct family (von Bertalanffy, which the
introduction already names but never uses; a Hill/Emax form; or a
generalised-logistic/Richards with a shape parameter that is not a pure
rate rescale), or explicitly demote tanh to "logistic at a steeper
rate" and re-frame the design as three families with a rate-robustness
check.

**M3. The pre-registered identifiability/sloppiness analysis (Study 2)
is promised but never reported.** *(Inspected: pre-registration
Study 2; absent from Results.)* The introduction devotes a full
sub-argument to Gompertz identifiability fragility and the Jagadeesan
sloppiness diagnostic, and the design section asserts Study 2 is part
of the programme. No eigenvalue spectrum, sloppiness ratio, or
identifiability result appears. *Why it matters:* a referee and a
reader are led to expect a deliverable that is silently dropped; this
is a scope-disclosure failure. *Remedy:* either report Study 2, or
remove the identifiability framing from the introduction and design
section and state plainly in the scope that identifiability is deferred.
The former is strongly preferred, because the identifiability angle is
the more original half of the stated contribution.

**M4. The executed design departs from the pre-registration in ways
not logged.** *(Inspected: pre-registration vs. driver and summary.)*
The pre-registration fixes a 144-cell Study 1 (4 families x 3 N x 3
effect sizes x 4 carryover levels), n_reps = 5,000 for type I error
cells, calibration by `optim` matching marginal on-drug Cor(B,BR) at
week 8, and per-replicate seeding "via cell-descriptor hash; base seed
in `01-base-seed.txt`". The executed study instead runs 16 cells, adds
DGP architecture as a *new* crossing factor that the pre-registered
grid did not contain, uses n_reps = 1,000 for the null cells (not
5,000), calibrates by single-anchor `uniroot` at week 5 to the BR
*level* (not the correlation at week 8), and seeds by arithmetic
offsets from `set.seed(20260509)`. The pre-registration promises a
`02-deviations-log.md`; it does not exist in the directory. *Why it
matters:* the manuscript leans on pre-registration as a credibility
signal (Section 4, Reproducibility), but the realised study differs on
the cell grid, the replicate counts, the calibration target and method,
and the seeding scheme, none documented as deviations. The type I error
cells in particular are under-powered relative to plan (MCSE ~0.005 at
1,000 reps vs. ~0.0023 at the planned 5,000). *Remedy:* create the
deviations log, reconcile the calibration description with what was
done, and either restore n_reps = 5,000 for the null cells or justify
the reduction. Note also that adding "architecture" as a factor is not
a "focused subset" of the 144-cell grid (as the manuscript frames it)
but a redesign; describe it as such.

**M5. The manuscript claims results are dynamically read back into the
text, but they are hard-coded.** *(Inspected: report.Rmd contains only
`setup` and `footer` code chunks; all tables are static markdown.)*
Section 4 states calibrated parameters are "read back by the manuscript
chunks for transparency", and the framing implies computed results.
There are no computational chunks; every number in the power, type I
error, and bias tables is typed by hand. *Why it matters:* hand-typed
results can silently drift from the data on re-run, defeating the
reproducibility claim; and the specific reproducibility statement is
inaccurate. *Remedy:* either wire the tables to read
`07-gompertz-summary.txt` / `07-gompertz-replicates.rds` via live
chunks (strongly preferred for a reproducibility-forward paper), or
delete the "read back by chunks" claim and state that the tables are
transcribed from the committed summary.

## 5. Minor comments

**m1. The "MCSE 0.013 to 0.016" range is wrong; realised cell MCSE
tops out at 0.0135.** *(Verified against summary.)* The abstract,
results, discussion, and conclusions repeat "MCSE 0.013 to 0.016". The
0.016 is the theoretical maximum at p = 0.5 (driver comment, line 7),
not a realised value. The alternative cells have mcse_power in
[0.0125, 0.0135]; the null cells [0.0049, 0.0059]. Report the realised
range (about 0.012 to 0.014 for the alternative cells).

**m2. The "roughly two MCSE" gap for logistic uses the single-cell
MCSE, not the SE of the difference.** *(Verified.)* The Gompertz-vs-
logistic MVN gap is 0.025; the SE of that difference is
sqrt(0.0131^2 + 0.0125^2) = 0.018, so the gap is ~1.4 SE (p ~ 0.17),
not ~2 MCSE. The conclusion (marginal, not reproduced) is unaffected,
but the arithmetic should compare against the difference SE.

**m3. Von Bertalanffy is named as a compared family but never used.**
*(Inspected.)* Section 1 (contribution statement) lists "the von
Bertalanffy growth curve" among the alternatives; the study uses
logistic, tanh, and piecewise-linear. Correct the list or substitute
the family (see M2).

**m4. The claim that tanh "has been used in pharmacokinetic modelling
for drugs with rapid receptor saturation" is unsupported.** *(Inspected;
no citation.)* The PK saturation standard is Hill/Emax, which the paper
cites elsewhere. Either cite a specific tanh PK application or drop the
sentence.

**m5. Orphan bibliography entries.** `corbo2005shelflife` and
`achiron2025piecewise` appear in `references.bib` but are not cited in
the manuscript body. Remove or cite.

**m6. Pre-registration source path is stale.** The pre-registration
footer points to `~/prj/alz/10-pmsimstats-ng/...`, an archive path
inconsistent with the current repository location. Update.

**m7. Document is in draft scaffolding.** The submitted source carries
the three-part `bullets / rgt / orig` structure with every `rgt` block
filled by Lorem ipsum placeholder text, and the `bullets` and `orig`
blocks duplicate each other's content. This is a pre-submission state,
not a referee-ready manuscript; the duplication roughly doubles the
apparent length. Resolve to single-voice prose before submission.

**m8. Self-citation transparency.** `hendrickson2020` (the framework
under evaluation) shares authorship with the manuscript's author group.
This is legitimate and probably unavoidable, but the relationship
between the evaluating team and the evaluated framework could be stated
once for the reader.

**m9. Type I error described as "consistent with corCAR1 small-sample
d.f. accounting".** *(Suspected, not established.)* The conservatism is
plausibly a corCAR1 / small-N artefact, but the paper asserts the
mechanism without a supporting check (e.g. a fixed-correlation or
larger-N comparison). Either soften to "consistent with" framed as a
conjecture, or add the diagnostic.

I did not find evidence that any cited reference is mischaracterised in
the substantive claims it supports; the growth-curve and pharmacology
citations (Laird, Norton, Zwietering, Benzekry, Goutelle, Dayneka,

asserted "the Gompertz function is symmetric whereas the logistic is

## 7. Suggestions for strengthening the paper

1. **Make the family manipulation real (the load-bearing fix).** Re-run
   Study 1 with each family parameterising the subject-level DGP and the
   biomarker coupling, not a post-hoc additive mean shift. This is the
   difference between testing the hypothesis and assuming its answer.
2. **Deliver the identifiability half.** The sloppiness/identifiability
   diagnosis (Study 2) is the genuinely novel and citable contribution
   and would lift the paper above "narrow reassurance"; report the
   eigenvalue spectrum and the unified-Richards reparameterisation
   comparison the introduction promises.
3. **Replace the redundant tanh family** with a structurally distinct
   one (von Bertalanffy, Hill/Emax, or a Richards shape parameter), so
   the four columns carry four shapes.
4. **Restore at least part of the pre-registered grid.** A null at one
   cell (N = 35, c.bm = 0.45, t_half = 0) is fragile; the manuscript's
   own Section 6 lists smaller N, smaller c.bm, and non-zero carryover
   as the regimes where the answer could change. At minimum, add the
   c.bm = 0.30 and a t_half > 0 slice, which the discussion already
   argues are the most likely to break the null.
5. **Wire the tables to the data** so the numbers are computed at knit
   time, and add the deviations log.
6. **State the estimand's sign and magnitude in context.** The
   interaction estimate is ~ -0.23; a sentence relating this to the
   calibrated true effect and to clinical scale would help readers judge
   the bias claim.

## 8. Scope of this review

- **Verified (cross-checked against committed output):** every power and
  type I error table entry, the bias range (-0.230 to -0.239), the
  empirical SE range (0.074 to 0.082), the convergence rate (100%), the
  cell MCSE values, and the runtime (381 s) against
  `analysis/data/quick-sim/07-gompertz-summary.txt`. All transcriptions
  in the manuscript tables are accurate.
- **Inspected (read and judged):** the data-generating logic in
  `01-architecture-a-trajectory-sweep.R` (the basis for M1), the
  calibration code (basis for M2 and M4), the absence of computational
  chunks in `report.Rmd` (M5), the pre-registration vs. executed-study
  discrepancies (M4), and the bibliography for orphan/mismatched entries.
- **Verified algebraically:** the logistic/tanh family identity (M2) and
  the Gompertz inflection result `t* = log(disp)/rate` (correct as
  stated).
- **Not checked:** I did not re-execute the simulation in R; my MCSE-of-
  difference and collinearity arguments are analytic, not re-simulated.
  I did not verify the `buildSigma` Architecture-B covariance
  construction at the source level, nor the `lme_analysis` internals. I
  did not independently confirm the prazosin reference parameter values.
- **Literature searches run:** (a) N-of-1 trial simulation with
  trajectory/response-curve-family misspecification (no on-point prior
  work found, supporting the novelty claim); (b) Gompertz-vs-logistic
  growth-curve misspicification and power in mixed models (returns
  general model-selection and tumour-growth work, none in the N-of-1
  power-analysis setting). PubMed-specific search was not run; a
  biostatistical referee may wish to add one for N-of-1 design papers
  post-2020.
