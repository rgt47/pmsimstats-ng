\section*{Problem and approach}\label{problem-and-approach}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Problem and approach}

Validating a predictive biomarker requires an interaction test. In an
aggregated N-of-1 design every participant is observed on and off
drug, and the power of that test must be estimated by simulation.
Paper 01 asks what the data-generating process (DGP) of such a
simulation should be. The DGP is a multivariate normal model in which
the predictive biomarker is a correlation with the drug response
(Hendrickson et al.~2020). The paper takes the open elements of the
DGP one at a time, compares the candidate choices, and judges them
against three requirements: a valid (positive-definite) joint
distribution, an interaction that survives carryover, and flexibility
to impose stress conditions. The
choices are collected as the recommended DGP, a step toward an optimal
one. The Hybrid design is the primary case. All simulations use the
default parameter values. The biomarker is assumed throughout to be
predictive only, not prognostic; the prognostic case is the subject of
a companion paper. A separate section lists the differences from the
DGP of Hendrickson et al.~This summary reproduces every table and
figure of the paper. They follow the paper's order, except that the
table of matrices that are not positive definite, in the paper's
Appendix B.2, appears here in Stage 1. Each is generated by the same
code as in the paper.

the constant rule E9 power falls to the test size at every positive
half-life. Under the decaying rule power stays near its no-carryover
value at 0.1 to 0.2 week and declines smoothly after. The choice of
the decaying rule rests on requirement 2, an interaction that survives
carryover.

\section{Conclusions}\label{conclusions}

Three choices matter most. First, the biomarker-response correlation
must decay after discontinuation. If it stays constant after first
exposure, the interaction is absent among the post-baseline visits
under any carryover, and power falls nearly to the test size. With a
decaying biomarker-response correlation, power returns to its no-carryover value at
pharmacological half-lives and declines smoothly at half-lives of 0.5
week and above. Second, the serial correlation structure sets the
largest interaction the DGP can hold. A separable AR(1) structure at a
serial correlation of 0.7 raises the bound from 0.256 under compound
symmetry to 0.474. The value 0.7 is a convention, however. At 0.9 the
bound falls to 0.340, and the Hybrid design needs
154 participants at \(c_{bm} = 0.25\)
instead of 278. Third, a matrix outside
the bound should be refused rather than repaired.

At the closed-form sample size, G10 power was 0.844 to 0.936 in the
uncapped cells. We propose the recommended DGP as a reference for
simulation studies of these designs when the biomarker is predictive
only. Every parameter is labeled as a
default value, a choice or uncalibrated. Calibrating the serial
correlation, the drug-response SD and the symptom half-life is the
next step toward an optimal DGP.
