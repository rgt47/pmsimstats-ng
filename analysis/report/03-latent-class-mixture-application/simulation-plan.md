# Consolidated simulation plan: latent-class and mixture-model
# extensions to the carryover and interactions papers

*2026-05-06 16:35 PDT*

## Objective

Characterise the operating properties of biomarker-treatment
interaction inference in aggregated N-of-1 trials when the
data-generating process (DGP) departs from the multivariate-normal
(MVN) covariance form built into Architecture B, and when the
analysis model has access to either the standard `nlme::lme`
specification or a mixture-aware alternative. The plan extends the
carryover-sensitivity and DGP-comparison studies along two new
axes: a latent-class generative axis and a class-aware analysis
axis.

## Common infrastructure

Shared across all five studies unless stated otherwise.

- **Outcome model.** Fixed analysis formula
  `Sx ~ bm + t + Dbc + bm:Dbc`, with continuous drug indicator
  `Dbc = exp(-lambda * tsd)` off-drug and `Dbc = 1` on-drug.
  Random intercept `~1|ptID`, residual correlation
  `corCAR1(form = ~t|ptID)`. This is the existing pmsimstats
  baseline analysis.
- **Class-aware analysis model.** Joint latent-class linear mixed
  model via `lcmm::hlme` with two latent classes, biomarker entered
  in the class-membership submodel, and the same fixed-effects
  structure for the within-class model. Convergence diagnostics,
  posterior class entropy, and label-switching checks are stored
  alongside fitted coefficients.
- **Trial designs.** `OL`, `CO`, `Hybrid`, `OL+BDC`, generated by
  `buildtrialdesign()`.
- **Sample sizes.** $N \in \{35, 70, 100\}$, covering the lower
  end of the trial-relevant range, a midpoint, and an upper
  anchor for trend extrapolation.
- **Carryover axis.** $t_{1/2,\text{DGP}} \in \{0, 0.5, 1.0, 2.0\}$
  weeks. Where the analysis model is the MVN linear mixed model,
  the analysis-side half-life is varied independently across
  $\{0, 0.5, 1.0, 2.0\}$ to inherit the mis-specification axis from
  the carryover-sensitivity paper.
- **Replicates.** $n_{\text{reps}} = 1000$ per cell for power and
  bias estimates; $n_{\text{reps}} = 5000$ for type I error and
  class-count recovery cells where rare-event resolution is needed.
- **Performance metrics.** Power for $\beta_{bm:D}$ at
  $\alpha = 0.05$, type I error under null cells, mean and Monte
  Carlo standard error of $\hat{\beta}_{bm:D}$, coverage of nominal
  95% confidence intervals, convergence rate, fit time. For mixture
  analyses additionally: entropy of posterior class probabilities,
  modal class-assignment accuracy, AIC and BIC class-count
  selection.
- **Reporting standard.** Morris et al. (2019) ADEMP applies to
  every study. Each study has an a priori ADEMP table giving Aims,
  Data-generating mechanisms, Estimands, Methods, Performance
  measures.
- **Common code surface.** A `generateData()` extension is required
  to produce mixture, combined-moderation, and
  heterogeneous-random-slopes data; a single dispatch on
  `dgp_architecture` is preferred over four parallel
  implementations. The class-aware analysis is wrapped in a
  `lcmm_analysis()` companion to `lme_analysis()`.

## Study 1. Mixture-vs-MVN factorial

**Aim.** Decompose the gap between Architecture-B power loss under
carryover and the corresponding loss under a faithful latent-class
DGP into (a) approximation error in the DGP and (b) inefficiency
from a mis-specified analysis model.

**DGP cells.**

1. *Latent-class mixture DGP.* Two-class mixture; class membership
   $Z_i \sim \text{Bernoulli}(\pi(B_i))$ with logistic gating in
   $B_i$; class-specific drug-response curves of modGompertz form,
   calibrated so that the marginal $\text{Cor}(B, BR)$ on-drug
   equals the Architecture-B $c_{bm}$ at the same parameter point.
2. *MVN DGP* (Architecture B as implemented).

**Analysis cells.**

A. `nlme::lme` baseline.

B. `lcmm::hlme` two-class mixture analysis.

**Factorial.** A $2 \times 2$ DGP-by-analysis grid, crossed with
sample size, design, and carryover.

**Estimands.**

- $\Delta_{\text{recovery}}$ = power(mixture DGP, lcmm) -
  power(mixture DGP, lme).
- $\Delta_{\text{approx}}$ = power(mixture DGP, lme) -
  power(MVN DGP, lme).
- Null check: power(MVN DGP, lcmm) should not exceed power(MVN
  DGP, lme); a positive difference would indicate spurious class
  extraction.

**Outputs.** A four-panel power-vs-carryover figure (one panel per
analysis-DGP cell) and an ADEMP-formatted estimand table reporting
both differences with Monte Carlo standard errors.

**Dependencies.** Mixture DGP code path; `lcmm_analysis()` wrapper.

## Study 2. Mean / covariance / combined moderation triptych

**Aim.** Locate Architecture B within the broader spectrum of
biomarker-moderated DGPs and verify the prediction that combined
mean-plus-covariance moderation produces carryover-induced power
loss intermediate between pure mean and pure covariance moderation.

**DGP cells.**

1. *Architecture A.* Mean moderation only, as currently
   implemented.
2. *Architecture B.* Covariance moderation only, as currently
   implemented.
3. *Combined Arminger-Stein-Wittenberg form.* Simultaneous mean
   scaling (with coefficient $c_{bm}^{\text{mean}}$) and covariance
   moderation (with coefficient $c_{bm}^{\text{cov}}$). Calibration
   anchors: at one calibration point, the on-drug marginal
   $\text{Cor}(B, BR)$ matches Architectures A and B; the mean and
   covariance contributions are then varied along a one-dimensional
   mixing parameter $\alpha \in [0,1]$ that interpolates from pure
   mean to pure covariance.

**Analysis cell.** `nlme::lme` baseline only. Class-aware analysis
is not the question here.

**Factorial.** Three DGP cells (or, for the combined case, five
values of $\alpha$) crossed with sample size, design, and the full
carryover and analysis-half-life axes.

**Estimands.** Power as a function of $\alpha$ at each carryover
level. The pre-registered prediction is monotone interpolation:
combined-form power loss between A and B at every carryover level.

**Outputs.** A power surface over $(\alpha, t_{1/2})$ at fixed $N$
and design; sensitivity plots at off-axis $N$ and design.

**Dependencies.** `generateData()` extension to combined
moderation. Calibration of the mean and covariance scales to a
common marginal effect-size axis is a non-trivial sub-task and is
the first deliverable of this study.

## Study 3. Heterogeneous random-slopes DGP

**Aim.** Test the Verbeke-Lesaffre / Proust-Lima
heterogeneous-random-effects representation as a more biologically
transparent alternative to Architecture B's covariance-only
encoding, while holding the analysis model at the existing
`nlme::lme` specification.

**DGP cells.**

1. *Heterogeneous random slopes.* Two-component mixture over the
   random-slope distribution on the drug indicator: a responder
   component with mean slope $\beta_R > 0$ and a non-responder
   component with mean slope $\beta_{NR} \approx 0$. Biomarker
   enters the class-membership submodel.
2. *Architecture B baseline*, for direct comparison at matched
   marginal effect size.

**Analysis cells.**

A. `nlme::lme` baseline (the audit-chain analysis the paper
   recommends).

B. `lcmm::hlme` (for completeness; this study borrows the
   analysis-axis from Study 1 but with the random-slopes DGP rather
   than the marginal mixture DGP).

**Factorial.** A $2 \times 2$ DGP-by-analysis grid crossed with
sample size, design, and carryover.

**Estimands.** Power loss under carryover for the
heterogeneous-random-slopes DGP relative to Architecture B;
identifiability metrics for `lcmm` under the random-slopes DGP at
small $N$.

**Outputs.** Direct power comparison with Architecture B; entropy
and class-recovery plots from Study 1 carried over to this DGP.

**Dependencies.** `generateData()` extension to draw
participant-specific random slopes from a two-component mixture.
This is a smaller code change than the marginal-mixture DGP in
Study 1 and could feasibly precede it.

## Study 4. Three-regime breakdown grid

**Aim.** Map the regions of parameter space where the MVN
approximation to a latent-class DGP fails, and quantify the
failure.

**DGP cells.** Three latent-class DGPs, each varying one regime
axis with the others fixed at a 'mild' baseline.

1. *Class-separation axis.* Vary the drug-response gap $\Delta$
   between responder and non-responder class-specific Gompertz
   peaks. At small $\Delta$ the marginal response is unimodal; at
   large $\Delta$ it is bimodal. Operationalising metric: the
   bimodality coefficient of the on-drug $BR$ histogram.
2. *Gating-slope axis.* Vary the logistic slope of $\pi(B_i)$ from
   gradual ($\pi$ near 0.5 across the biomarker range) to
   near-step. A pre-implementation step is to add a thresholded
   mean-moderation variant of Architecture A, which is the natural
   comparator at the steep end of this axis.
3. *Class-varying covariance axis.* Vary class-specific residual
   variance and within-class autocorrelation (responders and
   non-responders differ in $\sigma$ and $\rho$, with class-specific
   means held equal). This is the latent analogue of
   `nlme::varIdent`.

**Analysis cells.** Both `nlme::lme` and `lcmm::hlme`.

**Factorial.** Three regime axes (run as separate one-dimensional
sweeps, not a full three-way factorial), each crossed with sample
size and carryover.

**Estimands.** For each regime, the location on the regime axis at
which the MVN-LMM analysis loses more than 5% nominal power
relative to the matched mixture analysis; and the location at
which the mixture analysis fails identifiability checks (entropy
below 0.7 or convergence rate below 90%).

**Outputs.** Three regime-specific breakdown plots; a synthesis
figure marking the joint operating range within which Architecture
B is a defensible second-moment summary.

**Dependencies.** Thresholded mean-moderation variant for the
gating-slope axis.

## Study 5. Small-N identifiability and type I error

**Aim.** Establish whether mixture-model analyses are usable at
N-of-1-relevant sample sizes, independent of any specific
moderation question. This study is a pre-requisite for the
substantive interpretation of Studies 1, 3, and the lcmm arm of
Study 4.

**DGP cells.**

1. *Single-component, gaussian.* Architecture B with $c_{bm} = 0$.
   Pure null for the biomarker-treatment interaction.
2. *Single-component, mildly non-gaussian.* Heavy-tailed and mildly
   skewed residual distributions on the BR factor. Used to
   replicate the Bauer-Curran (2003) overextraction finding at
   trial-relevant $N$.
3. *True two-class.* As in Study 1, at varying class separations.

**Analysis cell.** `lcmm::hlme` with class counts $K \in
\{1, 2, 3\}$.

**Estimands.**

- Type I error for $\beta_{bm:D}$ under cell 1.
- Class-count selection frequencies (BIC, BLRT) under cells 2
  and 3.
- Posterior entropy distribution under cell 3 as a function of $N$
  and class separation.
- Convergence rate and mean fit time for each cell.

**Outputs.** A small-N diagnostic table that tells subsequent
studies which cells are interpretable, which require larger $N$ to
interpret, and which should be excluded from the substantive power
comparisons.

**Dependencies.** None. This study can run as soon as
`lcmm_analysis()` is wrapped.

## Sequencing

The dependencies imply the following ordering.

1. **Phase 1: infrastructure.** Implement (a) `lcmm_analysis()`
   wrapper, (b) heterogeneous-random-slopes DGP path, (c)
   marginal-mixture DGP path, (d) combined-moderation DGP path, (e)
   thresholded mean-moderation variant. Items (a) and (b) are
   independent; (c) and (d) share calibration code with (b); (e)
   can be built alongside (a).
2. **Phase 2: pre-flight.** Run Study 5. Use its small-N
   diagnostic table to select interpretable cells in Studies 1, 3,
   and 4.
3. **Phase 3: substantive studies.** Run Studies 1, 2, 3 in
   parallel (they share no DGP and no analysis dependencies). Each
   consumes Phase 1 infrastructure and Phase 2 diagnostics
   independently.
4. **Phase 4: integration.** Run Study 4. The three regime axes
   synthesise findings from Studies 1-3 by exploring where the
   approximations they baseline against begin to break down.

## Cross-cutting requirements

- **Pre-flight identifiability.** Every mixture-DGP cell must pass
  a positive-definiteness and convergence pre-flight analogous to
  `analysis/scripts/figure4/05-pd-diagnostics.R` before entering
  the production simulation grid.
- **Calibration to a common effect-size scale.** The
  biomarker-treatment interaction is parameterised differently
  across the four DGPs (Architecture A as a regression coefficient,
  Architecture B as a correlation, mixture DGPs as a
  class-separation $\Delta$ paired with a gating slope, combined
  form as a mixing parameter $\alpha$). A single calibration
  sub-study, performed once and reused across Studies 1-4,
  expresses each as the marginal Cohen's-style standardised effect
  size at a reference time and drug state. Without this,
  cross-study power comparisons are not interpretable.
- **Null cells for every study.** Each study includes a
  $c_{bm} = 0$ (or $\Delta = 0$) null cell for type I error. The
  temptation to assume the carryover-sensitivity paper's null
  calibration carries over should be resisted: each new DGP needs
  its own null check.
- **Computational budget.** Architecture B at the
  carryover-sensitivity paper's grid scale runs in approximately
  $O(\text{designs} \times t_{1/2} \times N \times c_{bm} \times
  n_{\text{reps}})$. Adding mixture DGPs and `lcmm` analyses
  inflates per-cell time substantially; a budget estimate from a
  small pilot at one cell per study, performed during Phase 1, is
  required before Phase 3 commits.

## Deliverables

- A `simulations.R` master driver script under
  `analysis/scripts/latent-class-mixture-application/` that
  orchestrates the five studies through the standard
  `furrr::future_map` and chunked-save pattern.
- Five study-specific summary scripts producing the figures and
  ADEMP tables described above.
- A `report.Rmd` results section that organises findings by study,
  with the cross-study calibration sub-study reported up front so
  that downstream effect-size axes are interpretable on a common
  scale.
- Pre-registered ADEMP tables for each study, committed before the
  production runs are executed.
